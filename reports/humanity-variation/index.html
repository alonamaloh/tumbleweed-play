Humanity, variation,
and playing strength.

Can we make a family of more human playing partners—and estimate their strength without playing thousands of games?

We mixed a machine policy with a policy fine-tuned to imitate human moves, varied how randomly each bot chooses, and played a complete round robin against Ataribot level 10.

16players in the field
7,680completed games

The finding

Average move loss predicted the broad strength ordering very well. It was a less precise predictor of the exact winning margin.

01

Two parameters, fifteen policies.

Humanity H

Mix the two policies’ move probabilities. H=0 uses the original machine policy; H=1 uses the human-imitation policy; H=0.5 gives each equal weight.

Machine0 · 0.5 · 1Human imitation

Variation T

Apply sampling temperature after mixing. T=0 takes the most probable move; T=1 samples the mixed policy. Higher temperatures flatten the distribution and increase variation.

Consistent0 · 0.5 · 1 · 2 · 4More varied

The 15 policy bots evaluate the current position without tree search; mixed players evaluate both networks. The human endpoint is the full-network fine-tuned model. Ataribot uses its ordinary level-10 search.

Why is there no single overall Elo ranking?

77 of the 120 matchups ended 64–0. Every player in the stronger group beat every player in the weaker group, leaving no finite, unregularized overall Elo fit. The table therefore shows direct head-to-head estimates: 400 × log₁₀(wins / losses). “+∞” and “−∞” identify undefeated or winless samples, not infinite underlying strength. No artificial wins or rating caps have been added.

05

Did badness predict the results?

Yes, strongly for the ordering. Less precisely for the exact margin.

Before the tournament, we evaluated every legal move on 3,000 held-out positions. A policy’s badness is its expected cell-margin loss relative to the highest-scoring legal move. This averages over the policy’s entire move distribution, rather than sampling a single move.

A linear conversion fitted to these results—margin(A vs B) = 43.817 × [badness(B) − badness(A)]—still misses by 20.9 cells/game RMS. This is an in-sample fit, not an independently validated numerical forecast.

The only observed reversal is H0 versus H0.5 at T=2: the half-human policy had slightly lower badness (3.462 versus 3.551), but the pure machine policy led by 5.94 ±2.59 cells/game.

The matchups share players and openings, so these are not 105 independent validation trials. The wide grid supports badness as a coarse strength proxy; it does not yet establish equal strength for nearby equal-badness settings.

06

Method and checks.

Policies

The machine endpoint is TumbStone’s accepted rung-20 policy. The human endpoint fine-tunes the entire network to predict recorded human moves. Legal probabilities are mixed first, then raised to 1/T and normalized. T=0 selects the largest probability, with a fixed coordinate tie-break.

Games

Size 8, with 169 cells. All 120 matchups share the same 32 fresh openings and colour pairs. Each opening contains one forced random legal move. No pie rule, resignation or playing clock. Games end when ownership is fully decided under the CodeCup rules.

Badness panel

3,000 unique nonterminal validation positions from 265 held-out self-play games, with no exact input overlap with the human corpus. A fixed rung-24 reference scored 238,631 legal actions: 100 visits per nonterminal child, exact scores for immediate terminals. Positions receive equal weight.

Uncertainty

Game results pair the two colours and cluster all opponents sharing an opening. Badness errors cluster by source game. All “±” values and error bars show one standard error, not a 95% confidence interval.

✓

All 7,680 games passed the final audit. Every move replayed legally, all final scores matched an independent native engine, and all 830,826 neural-policy decisions reproduced exactly. Separate checks verified saved-file hashes, colour balance and statistical calculations.

The fixed tournament took about 53 minutes, followed by a 3½-minute replay audit. No games from earlier calibrations were pooled into these results.
